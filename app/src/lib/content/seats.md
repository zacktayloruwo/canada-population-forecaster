## How House of Commons seats are allocated

Unlike the United States House of Representatives, Canada's House of Commons has no fixed size: each province's seat count comes from a legislated readjustment formula, applied after each decennial census (years ending in 1, taking effect in years ending in 6), and the national total is simply the sum. The model implements the real formula and assumes it remains unchanged for fifty years:

- **Electoral quotient:** 121,891 following the 2021 census (up from 111,166 after 2011). Each subsequent decade it grows by the average of the ten provinces' intercensal population growth rates. A province's initial seats = population ÷ quotient, rounded up.
- **Territories:** Yukon, the Northwest Territories, and Nunavut get one seat each, regardless of population.
- **Senatorial clause:** no province can have fewer MPs than senators.
- **Grandfather clause (as amended June 23, 2022):** no province can have fewer seats than it had in the *previous Parliament*. The floor now ratchets forward every redistribution — before 2022 it was frozen at the 1985 Representation Order, and the amendment raised Quebec from 77 to 78 seats.
- **Representation rule:** a province that was at-or-above its population share under the previous order, and would fall below it, receives extra seats until it is proportionally represented again.

Because the floors only ratchet upward, slow-growing provinces keep their seats while fast-growing ones gain, and the House grows. The starting point is the 343-seat allocation for 2022–2032, in effect since the 2025 election (BC 43, AB 37, SK 14, MB 14, ON 122, QC 78, NB 10, NS 11, PE 4, NL 7, plus one each for YT, NT, and NU). See Elections Canada, [House of Commons Seat Allocation by Province, 2022–2032](https://www.elections.ca/content.aspx?section=res&dir=cir/red/allo&document=index&lang=e), and the *Fair Representation Act, 2011*.
