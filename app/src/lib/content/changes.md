## What you can change

- **Fertility** — scale age-specific fertility rates from 75% to 125% of the reference period's values (the original classroom version ranged from zero, a "Children of Men" scenario, to a doubling — a new baby boom). The change applies across all age cohorts.
- **Mortality** — scale age-specific death rates from 75% (an extraordinary medical breakthrough) to 125% (superbugs amid pervasive antibiotic resistance). In the model the adjustment acts on the 55-and-over cohorts, where nearly all deaths occur.
- **Immigration** — expressed as a percentage of the resident population per year (0% to 2%) rather than an absolute count, so the slider stays meaningful as Canada's population changes; the equivalent people-per-year figure is shown beneath it. It does not include non-permanent residents such as international students and temporary foreign workers.
- **Internal migration** — replay the reference period's flows ("current trends"), turn interprovincial migration off entirely, or pick one of two alternative geographies: *Urban Agglomeration* distributes movers in proportion to each province's share of the national population living in census metropolitan areas (2021 Census, Table 98-10-0005-01); *Resource Boom* distributes them in proportion to each province's share of primary-industry employment — agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining, and oil and gas (Table 14-10-0023-01).
- **Reference period** — which historical window (2006–11, 2011–16, or 2016–21) defines "current trends" for fertility, mortality, immigration profile, *and* interprovincial flows together. The windows tell different stories: 2016–21 spans the COVID-19 pandemic, during which international and interprovincial migration collapsed for roughly two years before rebounding and mortality rose, while 2006–11 captures the pre-pandemic, post-financial-crisis era.
